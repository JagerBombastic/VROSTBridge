Headset & stream

Your headset is detected when you start a session.

With eye tracking the sharp region follows your gaze. A smaller region or a lighter outside sends fewer pixels: less work for both PCs and the network, and room for a higher stream resolution.

Sharpening brings back fine detail when the picture is shown in the headset. It is separate from foveated encoding, which decides how much of the picture is sent. Applies immediately.

Recommended settings: codec, stream resolution, foveated encoding and bitrate on Auto, with the frame timing lock, re-aim, late-frame catch-up and performance balancing on.

About the stream size

The headset's refresh rate is used as it is set on the headset, unless a rate is chosen for an Index.

Stream resolution is the picture the host sends per eye, not the headset's panel. H.264 is limited to 2048 pixels wide per eye; HEVC and AV1 to 4096. More pixels need more host and decoder time.

Changes apply to your next session.

Eye tracking

Bigscreen Beyond 2e: the sharp region of the stream follows your eyes, so the rest of each eye can be sent with fewer pixels.

Set up downloads Baballonia by Project Babble from its GitHub release (about MB; MB once unpacked) and checks it. Baballonia is not part of VROSTBridge: its licence, the Babble Software Distribution License 1.0, allows personal, non-commercial use only. Setting up accepts it.

Calibrate once
  1. In Desktop Mode, start a session with Eye tracking on. Baballonia starts minimised: choose Close Bigscreen's own eye-tracking app, then start a session with Eye tracking on. Baballonia starts minimised: choose Show Baballonia above to see its window. It shows its calibration in the headset only while SteamVR runs on this PC, so start the session first; without one it shows on this screen.
  2. Put the headset on. In Baballonia's window choose the Eye calibration (the first one) and follow it in the headset, where it shows over the stream. While it shows the dot, keep your eyes on it and turn your head slowly, far enough each way (corners too) that the dot nears the edge of your view: Baballonia only learns as far as your eyes went, and the sharp region stops short beyond that. It then trains a model for your eyes for 5 to 10 minutes: keep the session running until it has finished. Eye (no tutorial) is the same without the explanations, for later; Gaze and Blink redo one half after a full Eye calibration.
  3. Stop and start the session: the sharp region now follows your eyes, in Desktop Mode and Game Mode.Stop and start the session: the sharp region now follows your eyes.

Until Baballonia is calibrated the fixed Balanced region is used. Baballonia uses one to two CPU cores while it runs; on a handheld whose processor and graphics share one power limit, raise the TDP limit.

VROSTBridge sets Baballonia's cameras, its output (127.0.0.1 port 9005, this PC only) and, once, the left and right eye areas. Its other settings, calibration and the choice to share eye data with Project Babble stay yours (in ~/.config/ProjectBabble).VROSTBridge sets Baballonia's cameras, its output (127.0.0.1 port 9005, this PC only) and, once, the left and right eye areas. About VROSTBridge & open-source credits


VROSTBridge  · An open-source remote PCVR bridge. It connects a SteamVR headset on one PC to a rendering PC elsewhere on your network. Its streaming foundation comes from ALVR, with native OpenVR integration and these desktop pages. VROSTBridge is free software by JagerBombastic under the MIT licence (LICENSE); the software it includes keeps its own licences, credited below.
