Its own checks: the per-frame constants against the inverse with random edge ratios from 1.5 to 8 (at most 0.0006 texel, against 0.19 for 1.0.0.10's per-pixel terms), 1.36 million visible pixel centres from random meshes and outlines (none outside the spans), and the uniform buffer's layout as the shader compiler reflects it. Fixed from its findings before packaging:
  - the shown size could swing back and forth: on a PC that falls behind again about 40 s after each return to full size, its simulation made 36 changes in 30 minutes. A step up that is undone within 5 minutes now doubles the wait before the next try, and the third such return keeps the size down: 7 changes, then 90 % for the rest of the same simulation;
  - the seconds right after a step counted towards the next step down, and a second without SteamVR's GPU time counted as behind (the documents said it did not); neither counts now;
  - turning *Keep the frame rate* off could be overtaken by a size chosen just before it, leaving the client smaller with the switch off; choosing and sending a size now happen under one lock that the switch's handler also takes, and a test turns the switch off from a second thread while a size is being sent. A size that could not be sent is sent on the next check;
  - the `STATUS Lens area` line said pixels were skipped also where the fused pass does not run (the two-pass path, CPU conversion);
  - both GPU time stamps are now written once the work before them is complete: a stamp at the top of the pipeline could include the tail of earlier work, which mattered for judging the plane copy by its time;
  - a mesh without a lens outline to check its orientation against is now used both ways up (the review's unconfirmed suspicion), so an upside-down one cannot black out what the lenses show;
  - in the documents: the frame path in the tests starts at decoded planes (on Linux the VA-API import does not run), the output scale was tested with made-up TIMING seconds rather than earlier tests' lines, the pass's results match 1.0.0.10's to a hundredth of a level rather than exactly, only the pass's time is on the page, and the GitHub text now calls the third less work an estimate.
  - Left as they are: a change of size makes the output images anew, one per shown frame, which may cost a refresh (the back-off keeps such changes rare); and edge ratios between 1.0001 and 1.01, which no setting gives (the choices are 2 to 5), place a texel up to 0.75 off, against 0.39 in 1.0.0.10.
- **The frame pass's cost, estimated:** RADV's own compiler statistics for null devices of the integrated Radeon generations of handheld PCs (`RADV_FORCE_FAMILY`, `VK_KHR_pipeline_executable_properties`), per path with the region and detail forced: VALU instructions per pixel 232 → 150 in the reduced periphery, 254 → 198 in the sharp centre, 336 → 252 in the blend band, and the compiler's inverse-throughput estimate down by 34–40 %; registers 48 → 32. On lavapipe (the processor emulating a GPU, so relative only) the new pass ran about a fifth faster than 1.0.0.10's.
