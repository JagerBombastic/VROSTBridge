# Beyond 2e eye tracking on the headset PC (Bazzite and Windows)

Reviewed for VROSTBridge 1.0.0.14, 2026-10-18.

VROSTBridge sets up and runs everything around Baballonia, Project Babble's eye tracker: one **Eye tracking** switch, a set-up you start once, and a calibration you do once. From then on the sharp region of the foveated stream follows your eyes. Index headsets do not use any of it. The sections below describe the Bazzite client; a Windows headset PC works the same way with the differences in [On Windows](#on-windows).

On Bazzite the whole path has run with a Beyond 2e: the eye cameras at their full 90 pictures a second without losses, Baballonia's calibration shown in the headset over the stream, and the sharp region following the eyes. On Windows none of it has run on hardware yet ([still to check](#physical-acceptance-still-needed)).

## The parts
- **Eye-camera helper (Bazzite):** [go-bsb-cams](https://github.com/LilliaElaine/go-bsb-cams), included as source pinned to `d0df45de2d9ce1a7f7febf6a2c1313812e5df287` under its GPL-2.0 licence, with its HTTP listener moved to loopback and a small MJPEG server of VROSTBridge's own (`mjpeg_stream.go`) in place of a module whose repositories state no licence. A separate executable (`tools/go-bsb-cams` in the install); camera pictures never leave the PC.
  - It serves the Beyond 2e's two eye cameras as one side-by-side MJPEG stream on `http://127.0.0.1:8880/stream`, with the cameras at their fastest frame rate.
  - Pictures are passed on as the cameras sent them, only checked (one JPEG picture with a frame header and a scan; the standard Huffman tables added where a camera leaves them out). Decoding and re-encoding every picture cost a whole processor core on a handheld and queued pictures behind it. The first pictures after opening and one in 120 later are decoded in full; one that cannot be decoded is not sent, and one Baballonia's reader would cut short (the bytes FF D9 inside a header) or could not hold (over 900 KiB) is encoded again.
  - Each reader (Baballonia opens two, one per eye) gets each new picture as it arrives, at most one every 8 ms (125 a second), so the gaze is never held back by the helper.
  - A picture that lost USB data is skipped and reading goes on. The camera is opened again only when no picture has come for 2 seconds (5 after opening) or the device is gone, after everything is closed, with waits from 250 ms up to 4 s. The HTTP stream stays up meanwhile, so Baballonia keeps its connection.
  - Every 5 seconds it writes a `STATS` line (pictures sent and received, losses, rejections, re-openings, the longest gap, the frame interval and size), and a `CAMERA` line whenever it opens a camera. Reports carry them, and the card says *… samples a second from … camera frames a second*.
- **Baballonia 1.1.1.0rc6:** [Project Babble](https://github.com/Project-Babble/Baballonia), under the Babble Software Distribution License 1.0 (personal, non-commercial use). It turns the camera pictures into where each eye looks, owns the models and calibration, and sends VRChat-style OSC.
  - Not included with VROSTBridge. *Accept licence and set up* downloads the unmodified Linux release from Project Babble's GitHub page: `Baballonia.x64.v1.1.1.0rc6.tar.xz`, 517,439,412 bytes, SHA-256 `06c9ce7ecca19a0fee3ead40ef0c705ca173916e11a3315b99df63d348ea906a`, checked before unpacking and kept in `~/.local/share/vrostbridge/baballonia/1.1.1.0rc6`.
- **Gaze relay:** VROSTBridge's relay on UDP `127.0.0.1:9005`. It turns `/tracking/eye/LeftRightPitchYaw` or `/tracking/eye/CenterPitchYaw` into one combined direction and passes it to the native client's gaze socket during a session.
- **Gaze prediction (native client):** the gaze reaches the rendering PC 30 to 40 ms old on a wired link, and during a head turn the eyes counter-rotate to hold a point, so an unpredicted region lands behind where you look. Before sending, the client turns the gaze by the head's angular velocity over that time, bounded at about 20° so a glitch cannot swing the region across the view; a still head changes nothing ([technical reference](TECHNICAL_REFERENCE.md), *Foveated encoding*).

## Using it
1. Client page → **Eye tracking** card (Game Mode: *This PC* → *Eye tracking*): turn on **Eye tracking** and choose **Accept licence and set up**. It needs about 1.6 GB free while unpacking and 841 MB afterwards (on Windows about 1.5 GB while it installs and 877 MB afterwards). *Cancel set-up* keeps nothing.
2. Start a session in **Desktop Mode** with the Beyond 2e. VROSTBridge starts the eye cameras and Baballonia, minimised so that its window does not cover the page; **Show Baballonia** opens it. The card shows *Eye tracking is running* with the samples a second (up to about 66, depending on the cameras and the processor).
3. **Calibrate once, during the session:** choose **Show Baballonia**, then the **Eye** calibration in Baballonia's window, and follow it in the headset over the stream ([Calibration](#calibration)). Keep the session running while it trains. When the log says *Baballonia saved its calibration*, stop and start the session.
4. From then on every session, in Desktop Mode or Game Mode, runs eye tracking by itself. With *Foveated encoding* on Auto or Eye-tracked, the sharp region follows your eyes.

**Until Baballonia is calibrated the fixed Balanced region is used:** its general model is no guide to where you look. In Game Mode an uncalibrated Baballonia is not started, because its window (needed to calibrate) cannot be shown there.

**Camera permission:** the eye cameras (USB 35bd:0202) must be open to the signed-in user. If they are not, the card shows **Allow eye camera access**, which writes `/etc/udev/rules.d/70-vrostbridge-bigeye.rules` (the same rule as `config/70-vrostbridge-bigeye.rules`), reloads the rules and applies them to the camera, after the system's own password prompt (pkexec; one fixed script, the rule passed as its argument). Without a password prompt (Game Mode, or no pkexec), copy the file there as administrator and reconnect the headset. Uninstalling leaves the rule; remove it as administrator if you like.

## Calibration
Baballonia decides where to show a calibration when you start one:
- **SteamVR running on this PC:** in the headset, as a SteamVR **overlay over the stream, which keeps running.** Baballonia's own Linux way starts its calibration program (Godot) through OpenXR as a new scene application, and SteamVR then asks the application showing the stream to quit, which would end the session. So VROSTBridge puts a small launcher of its own (MIT, no Baballonia code) where Baballonia looks for its calibration program, `Calibration/Linux/Overlay/BabbleCalibration.x86_64`, and keeps Baballonia's program unchanged beside it as `BabbleCalibration.program`. While SteamVR runs, the launcher starts it with Baballonia's own `--use-openvr` option, the way Baballonia calibrates on Windows; otherwise it passes Baballonia's arguments on unchanged.
  - **The overlay needs a Vulkan layer of VROSTBridge's own** (MIT; `client/platform/linux/openvr_texture_layer.c`, layer `VK_LAYER_VROSTBRIDGE_openvr_textures`). SteamVR takes an overlay's picture through extension functions on the program's own Vulkan device: memory and semaphore sharing with its compositor, and timeline semaphores. Godot switches on memory sharing but not semaphore sharing; Windows drivers hand such functions over anyway, Mesa does not, and SteamVR calls them regardless, so without the layer the calibration crashes on its first picture. The launcher starts the program with `VK_ADD_LAYER_PATH` pointing at `vrostbridge-vulkan` beside it and the layer added to `VK_INSTANCE_LAYERS`. When the program creates its device, the layer adds the external memory, semaphore and fence extensions with what they build on, timeline semaphores and synchronization2, where the driver offers them and the program did not ask for them; it changes nothing else, and if the driver refuses the longer list the program's own is used. The list is the one [steamvr-room-setup-on-fedora](https://github.com/rcannood/steamvr-room-setup-on-fedora) found SteamVR Room Setup needs on Mesa, for the same reason. The layer writes one line to Baballonia's log per device (what it added, and any function SteamVR calls that is still missing). `VROSTBRIDGE_VULKAN_LAYER=off` in the companion's environment leaves it out.
  - VROSTBridge checks the launcher and copies the layer each time it starts Baballonia (so again after an update); *Remove Baballonia* deletes both with the rest.
  - If a calibration window closes by itself, Baballonia waits for it with its calibration buttons greyed out: stop and start the session (or close Baballonia) to try again.
  - `VROSTBRIDGE_CALIBRATION=openxr` keeps Baballonia's own OpenXR way, with `XR_RUNTIME_JSON` pointing at SteamVR's `steamxr_linux64.json` so it uses SteamVR whatever this PC's OpenXR runtime is (the card says if that file is missing). Without it no OpenXR runtime is forced, so with Monado or WiVRn Baballonia's own calibration keeps this PC's runtime.
- **No SteamVR** (*Open Baballonia* without a session): in a window on this screen. The card says so, and says to calibrate during a session when SteamVR was started by hand.

Baballonia has four calibrations:
- **Eye** (the first): a short tutorial, then the dot stays still while you move your head slowly around it, then about 20 seconds with your eyes closed, then training.
  - **Turn your head far enough.** The model reports only as far as your eyes went during this step: the dot stays still, so your eyes turn only as far as your head does. Keep your eyes on the dot and turn your head slowly each way, corners too, until the dot nears the edge of your view. Beyond the range you covered its answers fall short, and the sharp region stops short of where you look. Baballonia never reports more than 45° from straight ahead (its trainer's range), while a Beyond's view reaches about 55° to the sides.
- **Eye (no tutorial):** the same, shorter, for later runs.
- **Gaze** or **Blink:** redo one half, combined with the other half of an earlier full **Eye** run.

Training takes 5 to 10 minutes on the processor, by Project Babble's figure. Keep the session running until it has finished: stopping the session ends SteamVR on this PC, which closes Baballonia. The calibration's picture is drawn by Baballonia's program and placed by SteamVR's compositor; if the dot ever looks doubled or odd, stop the calibration and send a report.

## Keeping the gaze centred
A fresh calibration can sit off-centre: one run put the gaze 15–20° to the right, so the sharp region sat right of where you looked straight ahead.

- **Keep my gaze centred** (Eye tracking card and Game Mode; on by default). Your eyes rest near straight ahead most of the time, because your head makes the big turns. After about 11 seconds of a session VROSTBridge compares the middle of your gaze with straight ahead, and whatever lies beyond a resting gaze (3° to either side, or from 12° down to 4° up) is taken off every sample, in small steps, up to 25° sideways and 15° up or down. The card says where your gaze rests and how much is taken off, and when a calibration looks off-centre (10° or more to a side).
- The correction is **remembered for each calibration**, so the next session with it starts centred, and a new calibration starts with none. Only the two angles are kept, in `gaze-centre.json`, never a sample. **Reset centring** forgets it.
- **Use previous calibration** goes back to the calibration before the one in use: Baballonia keeps every trained model in its `Models` folder, so nothing is lost; VROSTBridge only changes which one its settings name (`EyeHome_EyeModel`), between sessions with Baballonia closed.
- Centring moves the whole range; it cannot extend it. A calibration that stops short near the edges needs calibrating again with wider head turns (*Gaze reach*).

## What runs, and when
- **During a session with Eye tracking on:** the relay, the camera helper (started first; VROSTBridge waits for its first picture) and Baballonia, all started at Start beside SteamVR and stopped at Stop. The relay opens before Baballonia and closes after it, because Baballonia's OSC sender stops for good after one refused send. A Start during Baballonia's 3-second stop keeps everything running.
- **Open Baballonia** keeps it running outside sessions until *Close Baballonia* or its window is closed. In a Desktop Mode session it starts minimised; *Show Baballonia* brings it back.
- **Process ownership:** Baballonia runs as the user service `vrostbridge-baballonia` (systemd-run, `KillMode=mixed`), outside Steam's game, so gamescope never focuses its window; without systemd it runs as its own process group. Both it and the camera helper run under a small shell watcher (`WATCH_SCRIPT` in `launcher/baballonia.py`) that ends them if the companion is gone, with SIGTERM then SIGKILL after 3 seconds (Baballonia ignores SIGTERM).
- **The stream goes first:** Baballonia runs at `nice 5`, and its unit has a quarter of the normal CPU weight (`CPUWeight=25`), because niceness alone only ranks the processes inside its own unit. When the processor is short the stream's work wins; with time to spare Baballonia runs at full speed.
- **Recovery:** if the camera helper ends, Baballonia is stopped at once and both start again (its camera reader never reconnects and would spin a core). If no sample arrives for 25 seconds, both restart: at most once in five minutes, never during a calibration or the 15 seconds after it.
- **Closing:** a Baballonia closed by hand stays closed for the session; one started before this VROSTBridge is restarted; another Baballonia (not VROSTBridge's) blocks the start and is named.
- **The headset:** eye tracking starts only for a Beyond, or a headset not seen before, and stops once the session's headset turns out to have no eye cameras.

## What VROSTBridge sets in Baballonia
Besides the calibration launcher and its Vulkan layer, only while Baballonia is closed, in `~/.config/ProjectBabble/ApplicationData/LocalSettings.json`:
- **Cameras:** `LastOpened{Left,Right}Camera` = the helper's stream, read with `IpCameraCapture` (Baballonia's MJPEG reader; its OpenCV path crashed), started automatically.
- **Output:** `OSCAddress` 127.0.0.1, `OSCOutPort` 9005, `VRC_UseNativeTracking` on. `UseOSCQuery` and `AppSettings_UseOSCQuery` stay off, since OSCQuery would redirect the output to VRChat on the host. `AppSettings_SteamVRAutoStart`, `AppSettings_CheckForUpdates` and `AppSettings_UseGPU` are off; the first time, `ShowOnboardingOnStartup` too.
- **Eye filter, once:** Baballonia smooths the eye values with a One Euro filter (`AppSettings_OneEuroMinFreqCutoff`, the smoothing at rest, and `AppSettings_OneEuroSpeedCutoff`, how fast it opens up when the eyes move). Its defaults (0.5 Hz, 3) take a quarter to a third of a second to follow a small eye movement. VROSTBridge sets 2 Hz and 60 while the filter is still Baballonia's default or an earlier VROSTBridge setting: in a simulation of Baballonia's filter at 60 camera pictures a second, that reaches 90 % of a 5 or 10° movement after 33 ms, two camera pictures. At rest it passes a little more of the tracker's noise, which the host's comfort zone keeps from moving the sharp region. A filter you chose is kept.
- **Eye areas, once** (`LeftCamera.Roi`, `RightCamera.Roi`): the two halves of the side-by-side picture, as Baballonia's own *select the entire frame* does for the Beyond 2e. Areas you chose are kept unless they no longer fit. What was set up is remembered in `~/.local/state/VROSTBridge/baballonia-setup.json`.

The first change to an existing file keeps a copy as `LocalSettings.json.before-vrostbridge` (an unreadable file is kept as `LocalSettings.json.unreadable`). Calibration, the eye-data sharing choice (off unless you opt in) and everything else stay Baballonia's.

## Eye-tracked foveated encoding
With eye tracking following your eyes, *Foveated encoding* Auto (Beyond) or *Eye-tracked* moves the full-resolution region of each eye to where you look. The host's driver moves it; the headset PC restores the full image with the centres the host used.
- The decision is made at Start: the region follows the eyes only when Eye tracking is on, the headset is a Beyond and Baballonia is calibrated; otherwise *Eye-tracked* falls back to Balanced. The note under the settings and the Game Mode row say which is missing. A change during a session applies at the next Start.
- The region is 40 % × 40 % of each eye by default (*Sharp region* Small, Medium or Large), with the outside at a quarter of the resolution (*Outside the sharp region*).
- **Comfort zone** (driver patch 0019, `gaze_comfort` 0.5): the region stays still while your gaze stays within half the distance from its middle to its edge, and moves to the gaze at once when you look further. A region that chases every small eye movement is visible as it moves; in a simulated minute of eye movements it moved under once a second instead of three times, with the gaze kept well inside its edge.
- **Hold** (patch 0017, `gaze_hold_ms` 1000): when the gaze pauses (no sample for 150 ms: a blink, or Baballonia skipping pictures) the region stays where you last looked for another second, and only then returns straight ahead (lost tracking, a stopped Baballonia). Returning at once left the region in the middle for a large share of frames.
- **The reduced area** (patch 0016): the host fills it from a mip-mapped, anisotropically filtered copy of the picture, so each reduced pixel averages the area it covers instead of sampling one point and sparkling. The headset PC enlarges it with a smooth cubic B-spline, and the sharp region with Catmull-Rom, blended between.
- **How fast the region follows:** the camera rate, Baballonia's inference and filter, then the stream itself: a picture is shown about 40 to 50 ms after the host encoded it at a steady 75 fps, and 80 to 100 ms when the headset PC shows the stream at half rate. The client's prediction through the head's turn covers the stream's part during head movement; keep the stream at a size the headset PC shows at its full refresh rate.
- **Gaze reach:** the Eye tracking card says how far your gaze went each way this session, in whole degrees (*Gaze reach this session: 18° left · 21° right · 14° up · 22° down*), and Game Mode has it as a row. Look to the far left, right, up and down, then read it; **Measure again** starts afresh. When one way stays under 30° the card suggests calibrating again with wider head turns. The relay takes the 1st and 99th percentile of each axis, so a stray value (a blink) does not count. The host logs the same per 5-second window (patch 0020, on `VROST_GAZE_FOVEATION`).
- The live **Eye-tracking view** (client page, host page, Game Mode) shows both eyes, the reduced area as coarse squares and the sharp region as fine ones with its outline, and the gaze point with a fading trail, so you can see the dot move first and the region follow. Below 30 samples a second it says the region reaches where you look late, and to check the processor or choose a larger sharp region. It is live only, never saved.

**The host** also turns ALVR's VRChat eye output on for such a session, so VRChat with OSC on, on the rendering PC, receives where you look. Foveation itself does not need it.

## Data contract and limits
- The relay accepts `/tracking/eye/CenterPitchYaw` (two floats) or `/tracking/eye/LeftRightPitchYaw` (four floats), in degrees, and immediate OSC bundles. Scheduled bundles, non-finite values and malformed packets are invalid; Baballonia's other messages (eyelids and expressions, about seven per gaze sample) are counted and ignored.
- Binocular directions are averaged: vergence and per-eye poses are not carried. Eyelid and confidence values are not forwarded, and a missing sample is not a blink.
- The timestamp is the relay's receipt time, not camera exposure time. The native client's 50 ms freshness cutoff starts there; the host's 150 ms staleness and its one-second hold start when a sample reaches it.
- Where you look is never logged or saved; the rendering PC's driver log leaves out the sharp region's position while it follows the gaze. Reports contain the eye-tracking state, the relay's counts, the end of Baballonia's log and how far the gaze reached (four whole-degree numbers, and the host's extremes per 5-second window).
- While eye tracking runs, the camera stream (127.0.0.1:8880) and the relay (127.0.0.1:9005) are reachable by other programs on this PC. Nothing but the combined gaze direction leaves it, inside the stream.
- **Cost:** Baballonia takes one to two processor cores (its eye model 5 to 8 ms per picture on one core, plus its window and garbage collection). On a handheld whose processor and graphics share one power limit, raise the TDP limit; the session card says when a fixed GPU clock or the power limit holds the processor back, which starves Baballonia of samples. Client reports list the processor time it uses.

## Updates and uninstalling
- Baballonia lives in `~/.local/share/vrostbridge/baballonia`, outside the version folders, and its settings and calibration in `~/.config/ProjectBabble`. Installing a new version over the previous one keeps them, and the installer's last message says so.
- The uninstaller asks whether to keep Baballonia: **Keep** (the default) leaves it for the next installation, **Remove** deletes it, **Cancel** stops the uninstall.
- When a later VROSTBridge pins a newer Baballonia, the one set up keeps working, with its calibration, and the card offers *Accept licence and update to …* (Game Mode: *Update Baballonia*). The older one is removed once the newer one is in place.

## On Windows

The Windows client runs the same eye tracking: one **Eye tracking** switch, a set-up after accepting Baballonia's licence, one calibration, and the same relay, recovery, status, foveation, centring and gaze reach as above (`launcher/baballonia.py`, `launcher/eye_tracking.py`, `launcher/gaze_provider.py`). What differs:

- **Baballonia reads the eye cameras itself.** Windows lists the Beyond 2e's eye cameras (USB 35bd:0202) as an ordinary camera named **BigEye**, one 800 × 400 picture with both eyes side by side, which Baballonia opens through DirectShow (its `OpenCvCapture` source). So there is no camera helper, no stream on port 8880, no udev rule and no *Allow eye camera access*.
  - **Close Bigscreen's own eye-tracking app** first. A camera has one reader at a time: while that app holds it, Baballonia gets no pictures, and after 25 seconds the card says so.
  - VROSTBridge asks Windows whether the camera is connected before it starts Baballonia and every 3 seconds while it runs. If the headset is switched off or unplugged, Baballonia is stopped (its camera reader would keep a core busy) and both start again once Windows lists the camera again.
  - If Windows lists the cameras as *USB Video Device* instead of BigEye, eye tracking uses that name and the card passes on Bigscreen's advice: in Device Manager uninstall that device (under *Cameras*), then reconnect the headset so Windows names it BigEye.
- **Set-up from Project Babble's installer:** `Baballonia.Setup.v1.1.1.0rc6.exe` from Project Babble's GitHub page, 423,324,292 bytes, SHA-256 `dec2e8fd1e5c844662f99b47680d772a393f6eb2fdf4e4dd3c62853928b2c470`, downloaded only after you accept the licence and checked before it runs.
  - It runs silently, for your Windows account only, into `%LOCALAPPDATA%\VROSTBridge\Baballonia\1.1.1.0rc6`, below normal priority and inside a job object that lets it start no other program (left to itself it would start Baballonia when it finishes). Three of its program files are then checked against their SHA-256, and the uninstall entry and shortcuts it writes are put back as they were, so VROSTBridge's copy stays private, as on Bazzite.
  - **A Baballonia you installed yourself** is used instead when it is the pinned release (its version, where Windows records one, and those three files match). VROSTBridge never removes it: *Remove Baballonia* only stops using it. One of another version is left as it is, and VROSTBridge sets up its own copy beside it.
- **What VROSTBridge sets** is in `%APPDATA%\ProjectBabble\ApplicationData\LocalSettings.json`, with the same backup: the same output, eye filter and eye areas, both cameras set to BigEye (read with `OpenCvCapture`) and started automatically, and **the eye model on the processor, once**. Baballonia's Windows default runs it on the GPU, which the stream's decoding and SteamVR's compositor need on time; a later choice in Baballonia's settings is kept.
- **Running it:** Baballonia starts with each session, minimised without taking the focus, below normal priority, in a job object that ends it, its calibration overlay and its trainer if the companion ends. Stopping closes its window, so it saves its settings, and ends it after 5 seconds if still running.
- **Calibration:** Baballonia's Windows release starts its calibration as a SteamVR overlay by itself, so it shows over the stream; VROSTBridge leaves its calibration program as it is. The advice in [Calibration](#calibration) applies.
- **The gaze** reaches the native client on a UDP port on 127.0.0.1, chosen at each Start and bound by the native client for its exclusive use, because Windows has no Unix datagram sockets. Baballonia still sends to the relay on 127.0.0.1:9005.
- **Reports** carry the end of Baballonia's own log from `Documents\ProjectBabble`.
- **Uninstalling:** the copy lives outside the version folders, so an update keeps it. The uninstaller asks whether to keep it: **Yes** (the default) keeps it, **No** removes it, **Cancel** leaves VROSTBridge Client installed; a silent uninstall keeps it unless given `/REMOVEEYETRACKING`. Baballonia's settings and calibration in `%APPDATA%\ProjectBabble`, and a Baballonia you installed yourself, stay either way.
- **Bazzite only:** the camera helper, the camera rule, the calibration launcher and its Vulkan layer, `VROSTBRIDGE_CALIBRATION=openxr`, the systemd unit with its watcher, and `nice`.

## Physical acceptance still needed
- **Bazzite:** *Gaze reach* close to how far you look (up to 45°), and the sharp region following toward the edges after a calibration with wider head turns; the region landing on target during head turns with the gaze prediction; recovery after closing Baballonia and after unplugging and reconnecting the headset; the CPU cost at your power limit.
- **Windows (nothing seen yet):** with Bigscreen's eye-tracking app closed, *Accept licence and set up* downloads, checks and installs Baballonia without an entry of its own in *Settings → Apps* or the Start menu; samples from the BigEye camera at a steady rate; the **Eye** calibration shown over the stream with the session still running; the sharp region following your eyes and *Gaze reach* plausible; a Baballonia you installed yourself used without a download; the uninstaller's Yes, No and Cancel; the processor time the eye model takes.
