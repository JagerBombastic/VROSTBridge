# Beyond 2e eye tracking on the headset PC (Bazzite and Windows)

Reviewed for VROSTBridge 1.0.0.12, 2026-10-13 (1.0.0.10 to 1.0.0.12 change nothing here). On Bazzite, 1.0.0.9 gives Baballonia a smaller share of a busy processor, so the stream goes first ([Data contract and limits](#data-contract-and-limits)). 1.0.0.8 keeps the gaze centred for each calibration and can go back to the previous calibration ([Keeping the gaze centred](#keeping-the-gaze-centred-1008)). 1.0.0.7 brings eye tracking to the Windows client too ([On Windows](#on-windows-1007)); nothing of it has been seen on Windows hardware yet. In the first test with the headset (RC4.5) the eye cameras showed in Baballonia, and its calibration showed in the headset during a session but then ended the session (with SteamVR started by hand, with the eyes' pictures swapped). 1.0.0 shows it as an overlay over the stream instead, which keeps running (see *Calibration*). In the 1.0.0.1 test on Bazzite that overlay crashed as soon as it showed its first picture; 1.0.0.2 fixes the cause (see *Calibration*), which needs the next headset test. The rest of the path was run end to end in a sandbox with the real Baballonia 1.1.1.0rc6 and a stand-in camera.

From RC4.5 VROSTBridge sets up and runs everything around Baballonia: one **Eye tracking** switch, a set-up you start once, and a calibration you do once. Index headsets do not use any of it. The sections below describe the Bazzite client; a Windows headset PC (1.0.0.7) works the same way with the differences listed in [On Windows](#on-windows-1007).

## The parts
- **Eye-camera helper:** [go-bsb-cams](https://github.com/LilliaElaine/go-bsb-cams), included source pinned to `d0df45de2d9ce1a7f7febf6a2c1313812e5df287` under its GPL-2.0 licence. VROSTBridge changes its HTTP listener to loopback and, from 1.0.0, gives it a small MJPEG server of its own (`mjpeg_stream.go`, the same stream as before) in place of a module whose repositories state no licence.
  - It serves the Beyond 2e's two eye cameras as one side-by-side MJPEG stream on `http://127.0.0.1:8880/stream`.
  - 1.0.0.3: each reader (Baballonia opens two, one per eye) is sent each new picture as it arrives, at most one every 15 ms (about 66 a second). Until 1.0.0.2 it was at most one every 50 ms, and only the first to arrive after that pause (about 17 a second), so eye tracking followed the eyes that much later.
  - 1.0.0.5: the pictures are passed on as the cameras sent them. Up to 1.0.0.4 the helper decoded and encoded every picture again, which took 10 to 16 ms of processor time each on a handheld (a whole processor core in the 1.0.0.4 test) and queued pictures when it was slower than the cameras. Each picture is now only checked (one JPEG picture with a frame header and a scan; the standard Huffman tables added where a camera leaves them out). The first pictures after opening and one in 120 later are still decoded in full, and one Go cannot decode is not sent. A picture Baballonia's reader would cut short (the bytes FF D9 inside a header) or could not hold (over 900 KiB) is encoded again.
  - 1.0.0.5: the cameras run at their fastest frame rate. Up to 1.0.0.4 the helper committed the camera's *maximum* frame interval, which is its slowest rate.
  - 1.0.0.5: a picture that lost USB data is skipped, and reading goes on. Up to 1.0.0.4 any damaged USB packet made the helper open the camera again, without closing what it had opened, and eye tracking paused each time. Now the camera is opened again only when no picture has come for 2 seconds (5 seconds after opening; also while USB data keeps coming without making a picture) or the device is gone, after everything is closed, with waits from 250 ms up to 4 s. The HTTP stream stays up meanwhile, so Baballonia keeps its connection. The helper reads the USB data as 100 transfers of at most 32 packets, so the end of a picture arrives sooner, and sends each reader at most one picture every 8 ms (125 a second).
  - 1.0.0.5: every 5 seconds the helper writes a line starting `STATS` (pictures sent and received, pictures and packets lost, pictures rejected, re-openings, the longest gap, the frame interval and size), and a line starting `CAMERA` whenever it opens a camera (the frame intervals it offers, the one asked for and the one agreed). Client reports carry them; the session timeline adds camera pictures a second, losses and re-openings; the card says *… samples a second from … camera frames a second*.
  - It is a separate executable (`tools/go-bsb-cams` in the install); camera images are never sent to the rendering host.
- **Baballonia 1.1.1.0rc6:** [Project Babble](https://github.com/Project-Babble/Baballonia), under the Babble Software Distribution License 1.0 (personal, non-commercial use). It turns the camera pictures into where each eye looks, owns the models and calibration, and sends VRChat-style OSC.
  - Not included with VROSTBridge. *Set up eye tracking* downloads the unmodified Linux release from Project Babble's GitHub page: `Baballonia.x64.v1.1.1.0rc6.tar.xz`, 517,439,412 bytes, SHA-256 `06c9ce7ecca19a0fee3ead40ef0c705ca173916e11a3315b99df63d348ea906a`.
  - It is checked against that pin before unpacking, then kept in `~/.local/share/vrostbridge/baballonia/1.1.1.0rc6`.
- **Gaze relay:** VROSTBridge's relay on UDP `127.0.0.1:9005`. It turns `/tracking/eye/LeftRightPitchYaw` or `/tracking/eye/CenterPitchYaw` into one combined orientation and passes it to the native client's gaze socket during a session (on Windows a UDP port on 127.0.0.1, 1.0.0.7).

## Using it
1. Client page → **Eye tracking** card (Game Mode: *This PC* → *Eye tracking*): turn on **Eye tracking** and choose **Accept licence and set up**.
   - It needs about 1.6 GB free while unpacking and 841 MB afterwards (on Windows about 1.5 GB while it installs and 877 MB afterwards).
   - The card shows progress; *Cancel set-up* keeps nothing.
2. Start a session in **Desktop Mode** with the Beyond 2e. VROSTBridge starts the eye cameras and Baballonia, minimised so that its window does not cover the page (1.0.0); **Show Baballonia** on the card opens it. The card shows *Eye tracking is running* with the samples a second (about 17 up to 1.0.0.2; from 1.0.0.3, up to about 66, depending on the cameras and the processor).
3. **Calibrate once, during the session:** choose **Show Baballonia**, then in Baballonia's window the **Eye** calibration, and follow it in the headset, where it shows over the stream (see *Calibration* below). Keep the session running while it trains. When it has saved the model (the VROSTBridge log says *Baballonia saved its calibration*), stop and start the session.
4. From then on, every session, in Desktop Mode or Game Mode, runs eye tracking by itself. With *Foveated encoding* on Auto or Eye-tracked, the sharp region follows your eyes.

**Until Baballonia is calibrated the fixed Balanced region is used:** its general model is no guide to where you look. In Game Mode an uncalibrated Baballonia is not started, because its window (needed to calibrate) cannot be shown there.

**Camera permission:** the eye cameras (USB 35bd:0202) must be open to the signed-in user. If they are not, the card shows **Allow eye camera access**.
- It writes `/etc/udev/rules.d/70-vrostbridge-bigeye.rules` (the same rule as `config/70-vrostbridge-bigeye.rules`), reloads the rules and re-applies them to the camera, all after the system's own password prompt (pkexec). One fixed script; the rule is passed as its argument.
- Without a password prompt (Game Mode, or no pkexec), copy the file there as administrator and reconnect the headset.
- Uninstalling VROSTBridge leaves this rule; remove it as administrator if you like.

## Calibration
Baballonia decides where to show a calibration when you start one:
- **SteamVR running on this PC:** in the headset. On Linux Baballonia starts its calibration program (Godot) through OpenXR, as a new SteamVR scene application. SteamVR then asks the application showing the stream to quit: in the first test that ended the VROSTBridge session, and with SteamVR started by hand the calibration showed each eye the other eye's picture.
  - **From 1.0.0 it shows as a SteamVR overlay over the stream, which keeps running.** VROSTBridge puts a small launcher of its own (MIT, no Baballonia code) where Baballonia looks for its calibration program, `Calibration/Linux/Overlay/BabbleCalibration.x86_64`, and keeps Baballonia's program unchanged beside it as `BabbleCalibration.program` (Godot still finds its `BabbleCalibration.pck`). While SteamVR runs, the launcher starts it with Baballonia's own `--use-openvr` option instead of OpenXR: the way Baballonia calibrates with SteamVR on Windows. Otherwise it passes Baballonia's arguments on unchanged. VROSTBridge checks the launcher each time it starts Baballonia; *Remove Baballonia* deletes both files with the rest.
  - **From 1.0.0.2 the overlay gets a Vulkan layer of VROSTBridge's own** (MIT; `client/platform/linux/openvr_texture_layer.c`). SteamVR takes an overlay's picture through extension functions on the program's own Vulkan device: sharing memory and semaphores with its compositor (file descriptors), and timeline semaphores. The calibration program (Godot 4.5) switches on memory sharing but not semaphore sharing. Windows drivers hand such functions over anyway; Mesa returns none for an extension the program did not enable, and SteamVR called it regardless. In the 1.0.0.1 test the calibration window flashed and closed, in a session and with SteamVR alone; the crash dumps showed a call to address 0 from SteamVR's `vrclient.so` inside `SetOverlayTexture`, and Baballonia kept waiting for the dead calibration with its buttons greyed out. As an overlay, the launcher now starts the program with `VK_ADD_LAYER_PATH` pointing at `vrostbridge-vulkan` beside it and `VK_INSTANCE_LAYERS=VK_LAYER_VROSTBRIDGE_openvr_textures` (added before any you set). When the program creates its Vulkan device, the layer adds the external memory, semaphore and fence extensions with what they build on, timeline semaphores and synchronization2 (with their features), where the driver offers them and the program did not ask for them. It changes nothing else, and if the driver refuses the longer list the program's own is used. The extension list is the one [steamvr-room-setup-on-fedora](https://github.com/rcannood/steamvr-room-setup-on-fedora) found SteamVR Room Setup needs on Fedora's Mesa, for the same reason. The layer writes one line to Baballonia's log per device: what it added and any function SteamVR calls that is still missing. VROSTBridge copies the layer from its own installation into the calibration folder each time it starts Baballonia (again after an update); *Remove Baballonia* deletes it with the rest. `VROSTBRIDGE_VULKAN_LAYER=off` in the companion's environment leaves it out.
  - If a calibration window closes by itself, Baballonia waits for it with its calibration buttons greyed out: stop and start the session (or close Baballonia) to try again.
  - `VROSTBRIDGE_CALIBRATION=openxr` in the companion's environment keeps Baballonia's own OpenXR way. For that, VROSTBridge starts Baballonia with `XR_RUNTIME_JSON` pointing at SteamVR's `steamxr_linux64.json`, so it uses SteamVR whatever this PC's OpenXR runtime is; if that file is missing, the card says so. Without that setting no OpenXR runtime is forced: the overlay does not use OpenXR, and with Monado or WiVRn (no SteamVR) Baballonia's own calibration keeps this PC's runtime.
- **No SteamVR** (for example *Open Baballonia* without a session): in a window on this screen. The card says so while Baballonia runs without SteamVR, and says to calibrate during a session when SteamVR was started by hand.

Baballonia has four calibrations:
- **Eye** (the first): a short tutorial, then the dot stays still while you move your head slowly around it, then about 20 seconds with your eyes closed, then training. Use it the first time.
  - **Turn your head far enough (1.0.0.6).** Baballonia's model reports only as far as your eyes went during this step: the dot stays still, so your eyes turn only as far as your head does. Keep your eyes on the dot and turn your head slowly each way, corners too, until the dot nears the edge of your view. Beyond the range you covered its answers fall short, and the sharp region stops short of where you look; in the 1.0.0.5 test it followed only near the middle of the view. Baballonia never reports more than 45° from straight ahead (its trainer's range), while a Beyond's view reaches about 55° to the sides.
- **Eye (no tutorial):** the same, shorter, for later runs.
- **Gaze** or **Blink:** redo one half. Each is combined with the other half from an earlier run, so it fails without a full **Eye** run first.

Training takes 5 to 10 minutes by Project Babble's figure, on the processor. Keep the session running until it has finished: stopping the session ends SteamVR on this PC, which closes Baballonia.

**Known:** in the first test (RC4.5, Baballonia's OpenXR way) the calibration was the right way round during a VROSTBridge session but showed each eye the other eye's picture with SteamVR started by hand, while SteamVR itself was correct. The picture is drawn by Baballonia's calibration program, not by VROSTBridge. 1.0.0's overlay is placed in the scene by SteamVR's compositor, which draws each eye from its own position; the next headset test checks it. With swapped pictures your eyes would not look where the calibration expects: if the dot ever looks doubled or odd, stop the calibration and send a report.

## Keeping the gaze centred (1.0.0.8)
In the 1.0.0.7 test a fresh calibration put the gaze 15–20° to the right of where you looked: over the session it reached 40° right but only 5° left, so the sharp region sat towards the right of the view while you looked straight ahead.

- **Keep my gaze centred** (Eye tracking card and Game Mode; on by default). Your eyes rest near straight ahead most of the time, because your head makes the big turns. After about 11 seconds of a session, VROSTBridge compares the middle of your gaze with straight ahead. Whatever lies beyond a resting gaze (3° to either side, or from 12° down to 4° up) is taken off every sample it passes on, in small steps as the session goes, up to 25° sideways and 15° up or down. The card says where your gaze rests and how much is taken off, and says when a calibration looks off-centre (10° or more to a side).
- The correction is **remembered for each calibration**, so the next session with it starts centred, and a new calibration starts with none. Only the two angles are kept, in `gaze-centre.json`, never a sample.
- **Reset centring** forgets the correction for the calibration in use.
- **Use previous calibration** goes back to the calibration before the one in use: Baballonia keeps the model of every calibration it trains in its `Models` folder, named by date and time, so nothing is lost. VROSTBridge only changes which one Baballonia's settings name (`EyeHome_EyeModel`), between sessions with Baballonia closed.
- A calibration that stops short of where you look near the edges still needs calibrating again with wider head turns (*Gaze reach*): centring moves the whole range, it cannot extend it.

## What runs, and when
- **The relay** opens before Baballonia starts and closes only after it stops. Baballonia's OSC sender stops for good after one refused send.
- **During a session with Eye tracking on:** the relay, the camera helper (started first; VROSTBridge waits for its first picture) and Baballonia.
  - Everything starts at Start, beside SteamVR, and stops at Stop.
  - A Start during the 3-second stop of Baballonia keeps everything running.
- **Open Baballonia** keeps it running outside sessions until *Close Baballonia* or until its window is closed.
- **Its window:** for a session in Desktop Mode Baballonia starts minimised (1.0.0); *Show Baballonia* on the card brings its window back. *Open Baballonia* shows it straight away.
- **Process ownership:**
  - Baballonia runs as the user service `vrostbridge-baballonia` (systemd-run, `KillMode=mixed`), outside Steam's game, so gamescope never focuses its window. Without systemd it runs as its own process group.
  - Both Baballonia and the camera helper run under a small shell watcher (`WATCH_SCRIPT` in `launcher/baballonia.py`). It ends them if the VROSTBridge companion is gone, and stops them with SIGTERM then SIGKILL: Baballonia ignores SIGTERM, so it is killed after 3 seconds.
- **Recovery:**
  - If the camera helper ends, Baballonia is stopped at once and both start again. Its camera reader never reconnects and spins a CPU core once the stream ends.
  - If no sample arrives for 25 seconds while everything runs, both restart: at most once in five minutes, never during a calibration or the 15 seconds after it (Baballonia saves the new model's path 2 seconds after training).
- **Closing:**
  - A Baballonia closed by hand stays closed for the session (*Open Baballonia* brings it back).
  - A Baballonia started before this VROSTBridge is restarted.
  - Another Baballonia (not VROSTBridge's) blocks the start and is named.
- **The headset:** eye tracking starts only for a Beyond, or a headset not seen before, and stops once the session's headset turns out to have no eye cameras.

## What VROSTBridge sets in Baballonia
Its calibration program's launcher (1.0.0, see *Calibration*) and, beside it, the Vulkan layer for the overlay (1.0.0.2); with `VROSTBRIDGE_CALIBRATION=openxr` only, `XR_RUNTIME_JSON` = SteamVR's `steamxr_linux64.json` in its environment.

Only while Baballonia is closed, in `~/.config/ProjectBabble/ApplicationData/LocalSettings.json`:
- **Cameras:** `LastOpened{Left,Right}Camera` = the helper's stream, read with `IpCameraCapture` (Baballonia's MJPEG reader; its OpenCV path crashed in the sandbox), started automatically.
- **Output:** `OSCAddress` 127.0.0.1, `OSCOutPort` 9005, `VRC_UseNativeTracking` on.
  - `UseOSCQuery` and `AppSettings_UseOSCQuery` stay off; OSCQuery would redirect the output to VRChat on the host.
  - `AppSettings_SteamVRAutoStart`, `AppSettings_CheckForUpdates` and `AppSettings_UseGPU` are off. The first time, `ShowOnboardingOnStartup` is turned off too.
- **Eye filter, once per filter version (1.0.0.3, 1.0.0.5):** Baballonia smooths the eye values with a One Euro filter (`AppSettings_OneEuroMinFreqCutoff`, the smoothing at rest in Hz, and `AppSettings_OneEuroSpeedCutoff`, how fast it opens up when the eyes move). Its defaults (0.5 Hz, 3) took 235 to 350 ms to follow a 5 to 10 degree eye movement at 17 samples a second. While they are still Baballonia's defaults, VROSTBridge sets them once to 1 Hz and 10. With about 60 samples a second the same movement then takes 70 to 85 ms, and the gaze is as steady as before when you hold still (both from a simulation of Baballonia's filter in `tests/test_vrostbridge_1003.py`). A filter you chose, or a later change in Baballonia's settings, is kept. 1.0.0.5 sets 2 Hz and 60 where the filter is still Baballonia's or 1.0.0.3's: in the same simulation (Baballonia's derivative smoothing is fixed at 1 Hz) at 60 camera pictures a second, 1 Hz and 10 reached 90 % of a 5 or 10 degree eye movement after 83 or 67 ms, 2 Hz and 60 after 33 ms for both, two camera pictures. At rest it passes a little more of the tracker's noise (0.6 of it instead of 0.35); the host's comfort zone keeps that from moving the sharp region.
- **Eye areas, once** (`LeftCamera.Roi`, `RightCamera.Roi`): the two halves of a picture twice as wide as high, one pixel short, exactly as Baballonia's own *select the entire frame* does for the Beyond 2e.
  - Areas you already chose are kept unless they no longer fit the picture.
  - What was set up is remembered in `~/.local/state/VROSTBridge/baballonia-setup.json`.

The first change to an existing file keeps a copy as `LocalSettings.json.before-vrostbridge`; an unreadable file is kept as `LocalSettings.json.unreadable`. Calibration, filters, the eye-data sharing choice (off unless you opt in) and everything else stay Baballonia's.

## Eye-tracked foveated encoding
With eye tracking following your eyes, *Foveated encoding* Auto (Beyond) or *Eye-tracked* moves the full-resolution region of each eye to where you look. The host's driver (patch 0010) moves it; the client restores the full image with the centres the host used.
- The region is 40 % × 40 % of each eye by default (*Sharp region* Small, Medium or Large), with the outside at a quarter of the resolution (*Outside the sharp region*). Up to 1.0.0.4 it moved whenever your gaze left a 2.5 % dead band.
- 1.0.0.5, the comfort zone: the region stays still while your gaze stays within half the distance from its middle to its edge (and never moves for less than the 2.5 % dead band), and moves to the gaze at once when you look further (driver patch 0019; the companion writes `gaze_comfort` 0.5 to the driver's stream options, from 0 to 0.8). In a simulated minute of eye movements at 75 frames a second the 40 % region moved 0.85 times a second instead of 3.17, and the gaze stayed at least 9 % of the picture inside its edge (`gaze_wandering_inside_the_zone_keeps_the_region_and_leaving_it_recentres` and the other tests of `vrostbridge_foveation.rs`).
- 1.0.0.4: when the gaze pauses (no new sample for 150 ms: a blink, or Baballonia skipping pictures) the region stays where you last looked for another second (driver patch 0017, `gaze_hold_ms` 1000), and only then returns to each eye's straight-ahead direction (lost tracking, a stopped Baballonia). Up to 1.0.0.3 it returned at once: in the 1.0.0.3 test that was 39 % of all frames, so the area being looked at was often the coarse one. The host's `VROST_GAZE_FOVEATION` line counts the frames on the gaze, held and centred, the pauses and the longest; reports carry them, and the relay's pauses between samples by length.
- The decision is made at Start: the sharp region follows the eyes only when Eye tracking is on, the headset is a Beyond and Baballonia is calibrated. Otherwise *Eye-tracked* falls back to Balanced.
- The note under the settings and the Game Mode row say which of these is missing. A change during a session applies at the next Start.
- **Gaze reach (1.0.0.6):** the client page's Eye tracking card says how far your gaze went each way this session, in whole degrees (*Gaze reach this session: 18° left · 21° right · 14° up · 22° down*), and Game Mode's Eye tracking page has it as a row. Look to the far left, right, up and down, then read it; **Measure again** (or choosing the row) starts afresh. When one way stays under 30° the card suggests calibrating again with wider head turns, in case you looked further. The relay counts each sample's angles per whole degree and gives the 1st and 99th percentile of each axis, so a stray value (a blink) does not count. The host logs the same per 5-second window, with where the left eye's region went (`gaze_yaw`, `gaze_pitch`, `region_x`, `region_y` on `VROST_GAZE_FOVEATION`, driver patch 0020).
- The live **Eye-tracking view** (client page, host page, Game Mode) shows both eyes, the sharp region and the gaze point. It is live only, never saved.
  - 1.0.0.3: the headset PC updates it 30 times a second (10 before). The client page asks for it 20 times a second, the host page receives it 10 times a second, and Game Mode redraws it 20 times a second. The gaze point and the region glide to each new position, and the gaze leaves a fading trail of its last second.
  - The reduced area is drawn as coarse squares (the larger, the lower its resolution) and the sharp region as fine squares with an outline labelled *Sharp*, so you can see the dot move first and the region follow.
  - 1.0.0.4: below 30 eye-tracking samples a second the view says the region reaches where you look late, and to check the processor (the session card says when the power limit holds it back) or choose a larger sharp region.
- **How fast the region follows:** the camera rate, Baballonia's inference and filter (see above), then the stream itself. A picture is shown some time after the host encoded it with the gaze of that moment: about 40 to 50 ms at a steady 75 fps, and 80 to 100 ms when the headset PC shows the stream at half rate. Keep the stream at a size the headset PC shows at its full refresh rate (the client page warns when it cannot).
- **The reduced area (1.0.0.3):** the host fills it from a mip-mapped, anisotropically filtered copy of the picture (driver patch 0016), so each reduced pixel averages the area it covers instead of taking one sample. The headset PC enlarges it with a smooth cubic B-spline, and the sharp region with Catmull-Rom, blended between. Fine detail no longer sparkles there, and edges have no light or dark fringes.

**The host:** an RC4.5 or later host also turns ALVR's VRChat eye output on for such a session (the `session-gaze` feature), so VRChat with OSC on receives where you look. Foveation itself never needed the host's switch.

## Data contract and limits
- The relay accepts `/tracking/eye/CenterPitchYaw` (two floats) or `/tracking/eye/LeftRightPitchYaw` (four floats), in degrees, and immediate OSC bundles.
- Scheduled bundles, non-finite values and malformed packets are invalid. Baballonia's other messages (eyelids and expressions, about seven per gaze sample) are counted separately and ignored.
- Binocular orientations are averaged: vergence and per-eye poses are not carried. Eyelid and confidence values are not forwarded, and a missing sample is not a blink.
- The timestamp is the relay's receipt time, not camera exposure time. The native client's 50 ms freshness cutoff starts there; the host's 150 ms staleness and its one-second hold (1.0.0.4) start when a sample reaches it.
- Where you look is never logged or saved. From 1.0.0.6 the rendering PC's driver log also leaves out the sharp region's position while it follows the gaze (it notes it only for a fixed or optical centre). Reports contain the eye tracking state, the relay's counts, the end of Baballonia's own log and, from 1.0.0.6, how far the gaze reached: the session's four whole-degree numbers and the host's extremes per 5-second window.
- While eye tracking runs, the camera stream (127.0.0.1:8880) and the relay (127.0.0.1:9005) are reachable by other programs running on this PC, as RC4.4's camera helper was. Nothing but the combined gaze direction leaves the PC, inside the stream.
- **Cost (1.0.0.5):** in the 1.0.0.4 test the camera helper used a whole processor core decoding and encoding the pictures again; from 1.0.0.5 it only checks them.
- **Cost:** Baballonia used about 1.7 processor cores in the 1.0.0.5 test (50 to 57 samples a second from 90 camera pictures a second; the pages say one to two cores). It used about one CPU core in the sandbox measurements: its window about 39 %, garbage collection about 11 %, inference the rest. On a handheld whose processor and graphics share one power limit, raise the TDP limit. From 1.0.0.3 it receives up to about four times as many pictures; its eye model takes 5 to 8 ms per picture on one core in the build environment. In the 1.0.0.3 test it sent 54 samples a second with the processor at its normal clocks and 12 with the processor held at about 540 MHz by a GPU clock fixed at its maximum; 1.0.0.4 says when that happens (see the release notes). From 1.0.0.4 Baballonia runs one step lower in priority (`nice 5`), so the stream's own work goes first when the processor is short; client reports list the processor time it uses. That niceness only ranks the processes inside Baballonia's own systemd unit, so from 1.0.0.9 the unit also has a quarter of the normal CPU weight (`CPUWeight=25`): when the processor is short, the stream goes first; with time to spare, Baballonia runs at full speed.

## Updates and uninstalling (1.0.0.3)
- Baballonia lives in `~/.local/share/vrostbridge/baballonia`, outside the version folders, and its settings and calibration in `~/.config/ProjectBabble`. Installing a new version over the previous one keeps them, and the installer's last message says so.
- The uninstaller asks whether to keep Baballonia: **Keep** (the default button) leaves it for the next installation, **Remove** deletes it, **Cancel** stops the uninstall. Up to 1.0.0.2 the uninstaller deleted it, so install over the previous version.
- A copy that an installation before 1.0.0.1 left under the old folder name (`~/.local/share/<name>/baballonia/1.1.1.0rc6`, with the receipt VROSTBridge wrote) is moved into place by the installer (or at the client's next start), without a download. Neither the installer's message nor the log names that folder.
- When a later VROSTBridge pins a newer Baballonia, the one already set up keeps working, with its calibration. The card then offers *Accept licence and update to …* (Game Mode: *Update Baballonia*). The update downloads the newer release, and the older one is removed once the newer one is in place.

## On Windows (1.0.0.7)

The Windows client runs the same eye tracking: one **Eye tracking** switch, a set-up you start once after accepting Baballonia's licence, one calibration, and the same relay, recovery, status, eye-tracked foveation and gaze reach as above (`launcher/baballonia.py`, `launcher/eye_tracking.py`, `launcher/gaze_provider.py`). What differs:

- **Baballonia reads the eye cameras itself.** Windows lists the Beyond 2e's eye cameras (USB 35bd:0202) as an ordinary camera named **BigEye**, giving one 800 × 400 picture with both eyes side by side. Baballonia opens it directly through DirectShow (its `OpenCvCapture` source) and takes both eyes from that one picture. So there is no camera helper, no stream on port 8880, no udev rule and no *Allow eye camera access*: Windows lets your own programs open the camera.
  - **Close Bigscreen's own eye-tracking app** before a session with eye tracking. A camera has one reader at a time: while that app holds it, Baballonia gets no pictures, sends nothing, and after 25 seconds the card says so and tells you to close it.
  - VROSTBridge asks Windows whether the camera is connected before it starts Baballonia, and every 3 seconds while eye tracking runs. If the headset is switched off or unplugged, Baballonia is stopped (its camera reader would otherwise keep a processor core busy) and both start again once Windows lists the camera again.
  - If Windows lists the cameras as *USB Video Device* instead of BigEye, eye tracking uses that name and the card passes on Bigscreen's advice: in Device Manager, uninstall that device (under *Cameras*), then unplug the headset and connect it again so that Windows names it BigEye. Until then another camera of that name could be taken for it.
- **Set-up from Project Babble's installer.** Project Babble publishes Baballonia for Windows as an installer, and VROSTBridge pins the same release: `Baballonia.Setup.v1.1.1.0rc6.exe` from Project Babble's GitHub page, 423,324,292 bytes, SHA-256 `dec2e8fd1e5c844662f99b47680d772a393f6eb2fdf4e4dd3c62853928b2c470`. **Accept licence and set up** downloads it only after you accept the licence, and checks its exact size and SHA-256 before it runs.
  - It runs silently, for your Windows account only, into `%LOCALAPPDATA%\VROSTBridge\Baballonia\1.1.1.0rc6` (877 MB installed; about 1.5 GB free needed while it installs), below normal priority and inside a job object that lets it start no other program (left to itself it would start Baballonia when it finishes). Three of the program files it installs are then checked against their SHA-256, and the uninstall entry and the Start menu and desktop shortcuts it writes are put back as they were. VROSTBridge's copy therefore stays private, as on Bazzite: no *Baballonia* entry of its own in *Settings → Apps* or the Start menu.
  - **A Baballonia you installed yourself** is used instead of a download when it is the pinned release: its version (where Windows records one) and those three files match. It runs from where you installed it, nothing is downloaded, and VROSTBridge never removes it: *Remove Baballonia* only stops using it. A Baballonia of another version that you installed is left as it is, entry and shortcuts included, and VROSTBridge sets up its own copy beside it.
- **What VROSTBridge sets** (only while Baballonia is closed) is in `%APPDATA%\ProjectBabble\ApplicationData\LocalSettings.json`, with the same backup of your original: the same output, eye filter and eye areas as on Bazzite, both cameras set to the BigEye camera (by the name Windows gives it, read with `OpenCvCapture`) and started automatically, and **the eye model on the processor, once**. Baballonia's own Windows default runs its model on the GPU, which the stream's decoding and SteamVR's compositor need on time, so the first time VROSTBridge turns `AppSettings_UseGPU` off, as Baballonia runs on Bazzite; a later choice in Baballonia's settings is kept.
- **Running it.** Baballonia starts with each session (there is no Game Mode on Windows), minimised without taking the focus, below normal priority, in a job object that ends it, its calibration overlay and its trainer if the VROSTBridge companion ends. **Show Baballonia** brings its window to the front. Stopping closes its window, so it saves its settings, and ends it after 5 seconds if it is still running.
- **Calibration.** Close Bigscreen's eye-tracking app, start a session with Eye tracking on, choose **Show Baballonia**, and in Baballonia's window choose the **Eye** calibration. With SteamVR running, Baballonia's Windows release starts its calibration as a SteamVR overlay by itself, so it shows in the headset over the stream, which keeps running; VROSTBridge leaves its calibration program as it is. Everything in *Calibration* above about turning your head far enough, the training time and keeping the session running applies; then stop and start the session.
- **The gaze** reaches the native client on a UDP port on 127.0.0.1, chosen at each Start and bound by the native client for its exclusive use, because Windows has no Unix datagram sockets. Baballonia still sends to the relay on 127.0.0.1:9005.
- **Reports** carry the end of Baballonia's own log from `Documents\ProjectBabble` (a Windows program writes nothing to VROSTBridge's `baballonia.log`), with the same status and counts as on Bazzite.
- **Updates and uninstalling.** The copy lives outside the client's version folders, so an update keeps it. The uninstaller asks whether to keep it: **Yes** (the default) keeps it for the next installation, **No** removes it, **Cancel** leaves VROSTBridge Client installed; a silent uninstall keeps it unless given `/REMOVEEYETRACKING`. Baballonia's own settings and calibration in `%APPDATA%\ProjectBabble`, and a Baballonia you installed yourself, stay either way.
- **Bazzite only:** the camera helper, the camera rule, the calibration launcher and its Vulkan layer, `VROSTBRIDGE_CALIBRATION=openxr` and the OpenXR runtime check, the systemd unit with its shell watcher, and `nice`.

**Not yet seen on Windows hardware:** the silent installation with its job object and the entry and shortcuts put back, Baballonia reading BigEye through DirectShow, the camera checks with a real headset, the calibration overlay over the stream, and the processor time the eye model takes on the headset PC.
