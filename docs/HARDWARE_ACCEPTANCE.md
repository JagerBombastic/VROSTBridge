# Physical acceptance — VROSTBridge 1.0.0.12

## Tested so far

This is the hardware VROSTBridge has been used with, by its maintainer:

| Part | Used with | Confirmed on it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rendering PC | Windows, an AMD Radeon desktop GPU (AMF encoder) | Streaming to both headsets |
| Headset PC | A Ryzen handheld with integrated Radeon graphics, with Bazzite, on Wi-Fi 7 and on a 1 Gbit/s USB Ethernet adapter | Desktop Mode sessions; Game Mode in part (its full check below is still open) |
| Valve Index | With Index controllers and SteamVR base stations | Picture, tracking and controllers, locally and through the bridge |
| Bigscreen Beyond 2e | With Index controllers and base stations | Picture and tracking through the bridge (RC4.5); the eye cameras in Baballonia; Baballonia's *Eye* calibration in the headset over the stream and the eye-tracked foveated stream in Desktop Mode (1.0.0.2); tracking that feels good from moment to moment (1.0.0.3); true blacks on its OLED panels with 10-bit colour off, and the High size looking and tracking reasonably well (1.0.0.4); a more responsive sharp region with the eye cameras at 90 pictures a second, and black blacks with the host's 8-bit fallback (1.0.0.5); much steadier motion and picture with the bitrate held to what the picture needs (1.0.0.8); clean game audio, and a session of more than twenty minutes on Wi-Fi without a lost frame (1.0.0.9) |

Not tried: a headset PC running Windows (the Windows client is new in 1.0.0.7, and no item of [its list](#windows-client-1007) has been checked yet), NVIDIA or Intel encoders on the rendering PC, Linux distributions other than Bazzite, other headsets (including the other Beyond models), and everything this page lists as still to check. From the 1.0.0.2 test on the Bazzite PC: the calibration over the stream worked in Desktop Mode (the overlay that crashed in 1.0.0.1), and the eye-tracked foveated stream worked. The reduced area sparkled, the sharp region followed quick eye movements late, and the eye-tracking view was hard to read; Game Mode always said its headset display reservation was stale, and once the host's SteamVR would not close after SteamVR started a second server process. 1.0.0.3 addresses each of these, to be confirmed. From the 1.0.0.3 test, on Ethernet through a USB adapter: tracking felt good from moment to moment, but the coarse area at the edge of the view could be looked at directly, short lags came every few seconds, Beat Saber stuttered and turned grey at times, High resolution with eye-tracked foveation struggled, and Game Mode's D-pad and stick did not go where expected. Its reports showed the sharp region back in the middle for 39 % of the frames, the processor held at about 540 MHz (below its own minimum) while the GPU clock was fixed at its maximum under the shared power limit, and one video frame lost every 3 seconds or so. 1.0.0.4 addresses each of these, to be confirmed. From the 1.0.0.4 test: High looked and tracked reasonably well; blacks were dark grey with 10-bit colour on and black with it off; eye tracking seemed to lag and did not always register where the eyes looked, and the sharp region could be seen moving; Ultra still struggled; once no video arrived at all. Its reports showed SteamVR on the headset PC without a position for the headset at that time (still calibrating its tracking), so the host had nothing to render for; and eye-tracking samples between 10 and 55 a second with pauses of up to 10 seconds, the eye-camera helper using a whole processor core, and every session at 30 Mbit/s after a network test that found no clean rate. 1.0.0.5 addresses each of these, to be confirmed. From the 1.0.0.5 test, on Ethernet through the USB adapter: working pretty well; black blacks (the host fell back to 8-bit: its 10-bit check measured white at 297 of 1023); the sharp region more responsive (the eye cameras at 90 pictures a second for the whole session, none lost) but following the eyes only near the middle of the view; controller models meeting about 10 mm before the controllers did (not so on the headset's own PC); an automatic bitrate of 50 Mbit/s on a path that carried 300 without loss. 1.0.0.6 measures the gaze reach (Baballonia's calibration range is the likely limit), tries the 10-bit conversion as plain SDR, judges the network test without the host's own sending time and measures the controllers' prediction, to be confirmed. From the 1.0.0.8 test: motion and picture much better, with a few short stutters, mostly lost frames; the game audio lagged, broke up and crackled at times; one of two base stations was not woken. 1.0.0.9 addresses each of these, to be confirmed. From the 1.0.0.9 test, on Wi-Fi: the game audio clean, no video frame lost (packet repair rebuilt 48), short stutters and audio dropouts only during pauses of the Wi-Fi link, and the automatic bitrate stepping down to its floor for them; the menu button opened the rendering PC's menu only sometimes; Game Mode started SteamVR, but its compositor could not take the headset display; the same base station as in the 1.0.0.8 test was not woken. 1.0.0.10 addresses each of these, to be confirmed. The rest of 1.0.0.1 (its names and icons on both PCs) has not been reported yet.

## What to check

Use **1.0.0.12** on both PCs ([release notes](RELEASE_1.0.0.12.md)); over Wi-Fi, turn off Wi-Fi power saving on the client page and run the network test again first. Check:

- **The bitrate after a slow start:** with *Use recommended settings*, a bitrate that steps down early in a session goes back to where it started once the headset PC's GPU clock is held higher (the bitrate line ends *Last change: back to the start: the GPU clock was raised*), and short stalls from the rendering PC no longer step it down.
- **The GPU clock from the start (Bazzite):** the next session on the same headset PC says *GPU held at … MHz from the start, as the last session needed*; Steam's Performance menu shows your own setting after Stop.
- **The add-on warning:** the rendering PC's page names only add-ons installed from outside SteamVR (a headset maker's helper, for instance), or nothing.
- **The rendering PC's stalls:** in a game, play five minutes with eye tracking on and five with it off, with reports from both PCs: the host report's `VROST_HOST_ACQUIRE` lines and SteamVR's `WaitForAcquire` warnings say whether the stalls follow the game or the gaze.

Then 1.0.0.11's checks still open (its frame pass ran at Native with eye-tracked foveation in its first session, at 1.9 ms of GPU time a frame):

- **The picture's edges:** nothing at the edges of the view looks cut off or dark, also while turning the head quickly; the client log's `VISIBLE_AREA` line gives each eye's share of computed pixels, and its `STATUS Lens area` line whether the headset's mesh was used as given, turned, used both ways up or left out. If the edges look wrong, compare with *Skip what the lenses never show* off.
- **Keeping the frame rate:** where the headset PC cannot hold the refresh at Native, the page shows *Shown at 90 %* (or 80 %) beside the frame rate instead of the stream dropping to half rate, the log has a `SCALE` line with the reason, and the full size comes back once there is room; with *Keep the frame rate when this PC falls behind* off, the size stays at 100 %.

Then 1.0.0.10's checks, none reported yet:

- **The menu button:** short and long presses of the system button on either controller open the rendering PC's menu (or recentre, for a long press) every time, and holding a grip or trigger through a short press does not let go; the client log has a `SYSTEM_BUTTON` line per press.
- **Game Mode:** with the headset connected before entering Game Mode, Start from the Steam library entry streams, the headset PC's built-in screen staying on. If it does not, the message says why, and a report from either mode carries `game_mode_last_start`.
- **The bitrate on Wi-Fi:** it holds, or a step for uneven arrival comes back up.
- **Base stations:** all wake at Start; on Bazzite one that does not is named with its signal strength.

Then 1.0.0.9's checks still open:

- **Packet repair at High:** with eye-tracked foveation, *Rebuilt on the way* counts frames while lost frames stay rare and the bitrate holds.
- **Base stations off:** all sleep within about 10 s of VROSTBridge closing, on Bazzite and on Windows.

Then 1.0.0.8's checks still open:

- **Performance balance (Bazzite):** with the charger in, the *This PC* card says what was set; the GPU clock rises when frames run late without eye tracking slowing; Stop (or the next start after a forced quit) puts Steam's Performance settings back.
- **Gaze centring (Beyond 2e):** the sharp region sits where you look, straight ahead included, even with an off-centre calibration; *Use previous calibration* and *Reset centring* do what they say.

Then the earlier checks, which apply as written. For the host and the Bazzite client 1.0.0.7 changed only a variant of the frame shader for drivers with little push-constant space, the page's wrapping of long messages ([release notes](history/RELEASE_1.0.0.7.md)) and the way the host's companion lists the PC's network adapters (through Windows' own interface, with the earlier PowerShell query as the fallback); a Windows headset PC has [its own list](#windows-client-1007). The 1.0.0.6 checks (*Gaze reach* before and after calibrating with wider head turns, the sharp region toward the edges, the 10-bit attempts in `VROST_COLOR_PROBE`, the network test repeated and its bitrate, the controllers held still and tapped together) are in its [release notes](history/RELEASE_1.0.0.6.md#how-to-test-please-report-each); the 1.0.0.5 checks still open (black with 10-bit colour on, the network test repeated and the bitrate it sets, eye-tracking samples and camera pictures a second, the sharp region still for small eye movements and following larger ones, *Headset not tracked*, Ultra shown at SteamVR's size) are in its [release notes](history/RELEASE_1.0.0.5.md#how-to-test-please-report-each); the 1.0.0.4 checks still open (the network test run once more and fewer lost frames, the processor warning and *Set the GPU clock to automatic*, the sharp region through blinks and pauses, Beat Saber without grey flashes, Game Mode's D-pad and stick, High with eye-tracked foveation) are in its [release notes](history/RELEASE_1.0.0.4.md#how-to-test-please-report-each); the 1.0.0.3 checks still open (Game Mode past the display reservation, Stop closing the host's SteamVR, the reduced area, quick eye movements, the eye-tracking view, an update that keeps Baballonia) are in its [release notes](history/RELEASE_1.0.0.3.md#how-to-test-please-report-each); the 1.0.0.2 check (the calibration over the stream) in [its notes](history/RELEASE_1.0.0.2.md#how-to-test-please-report-each). The 1.0.0.1 checks (a fresh installation on both PCs with its names and icons, a whole session, Baballonia's calibration over the stream with the session kept running, Baballonia minimised, one page tab) are in its [release notes](history/RELEASE_1.0.0.1.md#how-to-test-please-report-each), the RC4.5 checks (eye tracking set up and run by VROSTBridge) in [its notes](history/RELEASE_1.0.0-rc4.5.md#how-to-test-please-report-each), the RC4.4 checks in [its notes](history/RELEASE_1.0.0-rc4.4.md#how-to-test-please-report-each) (raise the client PC's TDP limit first), the RC4.3 checks in [its notes](history/RELEASE_1.0.0-rc4.3.md#how-to-test-please-report-each), the RC4.2 checks in [its notes](history/RELEASE_1.0.0-rc4.2.md#how-to-test-please-report-each), the RC4.1 checks in [its notes](history/RELEASE_1.0.0-rc4.1.md#how-to-test-please-report-each) and the RC4 feature checks in the [RC4 notes](history/RELEASE_1.0.0-rc4.md#how-to-test-please-report-each). Save reports before restarting a failed session when possible: use **Send report to host** on the client and download it with the host's own report on the host page.

## Record each run

Record HMD model, local display mode/effective refresh, client/host GPU and drivers, the client's power limit (TDP) setting, its GPU clock setting (automatic, or fixed and at what) and whether the charger was connected, SteamVR versions, wired/Wi-Fi route (and whether Wi-Fi stayed on one access point), frame re-aim on/off and the alignment check answer, codec and bit depth, foveation preset (and whether gaze drives it), requested/negotiated/encoded resolution, bitrate (manual or measured), pacing rate, test scene and duration. Keep one variable at a time when comparing performance. Frame age is not measured motion-to-photon latency.

## Required sequence

1. Confirm correct local SteamVR view, tracking, both controllers and room setup on the client with the bridge stopped.
2. On Beyond, confirm RC1.3 stereo alignment, scale, optical centre and head-motion stability. If wrong, capture `view_geometry` and describe the distortion. Regress the Index using the same updated client.
3. Perform two cold starts and two warm Start/Stop cycles. Start only from the client. Check both runtimes close on Stop and the host returns to waiting. Check quiet host sign-in startup separately.
4. Run at least 30 minutes of a demanding game. Independently test left/right actions, trigger/grip, sticks, touches, finger poses, haptics and host dashboard. Sleep/reconnect each controller and confirm input recovery, not just pose recovery.
5. Adjust IPD during a stream and check geometry remains stable. Confirm the host rectangular play area and standing origin agree with the local setup; the local boundary remains authoritative.
6. Compare Wi-Fi and Ethernet at identical quality settings and with the intended adapter selected. Record unique frame cadence, repeated frames, frame age, host timing and client decode/conversion timing. Investigate observed half-rate episodes.
7. Exercise brief network interruption, loss of host/native process, cancellation during startup and retry. Check held/neutral remote presentation, actionable status and settings restoration. Do not deliberately disable physical boundary protections.
8. After desktop success, add VROSTBridge to Steam from the client page and validate Game Mode: the handheld's own screen stays on with the headset connected, the library artwork, the lease/display, native controls, host menu, Stop/Exit and return to desktop. Connect/power HMD before entering Game Mode.
9. If using Beyond 2e gaze, validate clear cameras, calibration after refitting, host direction, provider/camera restart and stale-data handling. With *Eye-tracked* foveation, look around without moving your head: the image must look equally sharp wherever you look, without lag, shimmer or a visible region edge. This does not qualify OpenXR gaze or foveated rendering in games.
10. Foveated encoding without gaze: compare *Off*, *Subtle* and *Balanced* in the same scene on each headset; look for edge blur, seams or eye mismatch.
11. Network and power: run the network test on Ethernet and Wi-Fi; compare pacing on/off on a slower client link; with gradual recovery on Wi-Fi check losses heal without freezes. Wake the sleeping host from the client, check it stays awake in a long session and sleeps after the configured time; check client sleep block and headset audio restore after Stop.
12. Test fresh install, upgrade, repair and uninstall on the actual platforms, including state retention and no damage to unrelated software.

There is no demonstrated universal latency threshold in current evidence. Each check counts when its result repeats; a short successful session alone is not enough. Results on hardware not listed under *Tested so far* are especially welcome (a bug report with both reports is the best way to send them).

## Windows client (1.0.0.7)

**No item in this list has been checked yet.** The Windows client has been built and tested only in code: the companion's unit tests, the native tests under Wine with a software Vulkan driver, the installer under Wine, and a smoke test of the companion and its page under Wine ([technical reference, §20.2](TECHNICAL_REFERENCE.md#202-built-and-tested-in-code-not-yet-confirmed-on-hardware)). Its decoding on a real GPU, Windows Firewall, Wi-Fi and power settings, Baballonia's Windows installer and SteamVR on Windows have never run. The [1.0.0.7 release notes](history/RELEASE_1.0.0.7.md#how-to-test-the-windows-client-please-report-each) have the short version of this list.

You need a headset PC with Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit), Steam and SteamVR, and the headset working in SteamVR on it (start SteamVR once with the headset connected, finish its room setup, then close it), and a rendering PC with VROSTBridge Host 1.0.0.7. The client's log is `client-latest.log` in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\VROSTBridge` (Start menu: *Client startup logs*); its report has the same lines. Send reports from both PCs with each result.

Record for each Windows run, as well as the items under *Record each run*: the Windows edition, version and build; the headset PC's GPU and its driver version (and which GPU drives the headset if there are two); the client's `STATUS SteamVR's GPU for D3D11VA` and `STATUS Video decoding` lines; whether the PC was on its charger; Wi-Fi or Ethernet.

1. Run `VROSTBridge-Client-Setup-1.0.0.7.exe`. It is not code-signed, so Windows SmartScreen may say *Windows protected your PC*: choose **More info → Run anyway**, and record whether it asked. It passes when it installs without an administrator prompt into `%LOCALAPPDATA%\VROSTBridge\Client\1.0.0.7`, the Start menu has *VROSTBridge Client*, the page shows *VROSTBridge Client 1.0.0.7*, and running the setup again (a repair) keeps settings and pairing. `VROSTBridge-Client-Portable-1.0.0.7.zip`, unpacked whole, opens the same page from its `VROSTBridge Client.exe`. On a PC where SteamVR has never run, if you have one, Start must stop with *SteamVR is not set up on this PC yet*.
2. Press **Allow home-network connection** and accept the one administrator (UAC) prompt. It passes when the page's card then reads *Home-network firewall rules are in place (private networks, local subnet only)*, and Windows Defender Firewall's inbound rules hold exactly five `VROSTBridge.Client.* (private LAN)` rules, each for the Private profile and the local subnet: TCP 9943, UDP 9944 and UDP 5353 for `VROSTBridgeClient.exe`, and TCP 9945 and UDP 9944 and 9946 for the companion's `python.exe`. Declining the prompt must change nothing and say so; on a network Windows treats as Public, the card must say that the rules do not apply there.
3. Discovery and pairing: with *Allow home-network clients* on in the host page, start a session on the client. It passes when the client finds the host by itself, without an address typed, pairs, and the host page lists it; the next Start reuses the pairing.
4. D3D11VA decoding on SteamVR's GPU: with *CPU decoding* off, start a session. It passes when the client's log has `STATUS SteamVR's GPU for D3D11VA: <GPU> (DXGI adapter <n>); decoder profiles h264=… hevc=… hevc_main10=… av1=… av1_10bit=…`, then `STATUS Video decoding: D3D11VA on <GPU>, copied to Vulkan` and `STATUS Video decoder opened successfully: backend=D3D11VA …`, all naming the GPU the headset is connected to (on a PC with two GPUs, the one SteamVR renders with), the page's video details say *decoded with D3D11VA*, and the picture in the headset is right. The adapter is matched by its LUID, which the log does not print: its name and DXGI number identify it. Record the decoder profiles line. `STATUS SteamVR's GPU for D3D11VA not found`, `STATUS Video decoding: D3D11VA unavailable` or a `FATAL` D3D11VA line is a failure. Then turn on *CPU decoding* and start an H.264 or HEVC session: the log must say `STATUS Video decoding: CPU, uploaded to Vulkan` and the picture must be right (without foveation); record its frame rate, as CPU decoding is a troubleshooting fallback.
5. Codecs and 10-bit: the client offers the host only what this GPU's decoder handles (`STATUS Stream features offered: foveated=… ten_bit=… av1=…`). Stream H.264 (at *Standard*), HEVC and, where offered, AV1 and 10-bit colour, a few minutes each. It passes when every offered combination gives a correct picture: the `VIDEO` line names the codec with `"decoder":"d3d11va"`, a 10-bit stream's `VIDEO_COLOUR` line says `"bits":10`, blacks are black and nothing is corrupted. Choosing AV1 where the line says `av1=0` must give an HEVC stream from the host, not a broken one.
6. The copy's cost: at the size you would use, run the same scene for five minutes with foveation *Off* and again with *Balanced* (or *Eye-tracked* on a Beyond 2e). For both runs record, from the client's TIMING lines, `map_sync_import_mean_ms`, `map_sync_import_p95_ms` and `map_sync_import_max_ms` (the copy of each picture into Vulkan), `unique_fps` against `refresh_hz`, and the lost frames. It passes when the stream holds the headset's refresh rate (`unique_fps` at `refresh_hz`) at that size. A copy whose p95 takes a large part of the frame interval (13.3 ms at 75 Hz), or a frame rate clearly higher with foveation than without, is the evidence for sharing the picture without the copy.
7. The frame pass: from 1.0.0.11 one shader serves every GPU (its constants in a uniform buffer, none as push constants, so a driver with only the minimum push-constant space runs it too). The log must say `STATUS Fused frame pass ready: …` and `STATUS Frame pass: fused (one pass per shown frame); sharpening …`, and `STATUS GPU time stamps: on (…)` where the GPU's queue has time stamps. It passes when the picture, its sharpening and the re-aim are right and the `TIMING` lines carry `frame_pass_gpu` and `plane_copy_gpu`; record both with item 6's numbers.
8. Re-aim and controllers: on the Windows PC, do items 1, 3 and 4 of the *Required sequence* above (local SteamVR first, cold and warm Start/Stop, a 30-minute session with every control) and the page's alignment check. It passes when head turns keep head-locked text and held objects steady, both controllers' buttons, sticks, touches, finger poses, haptics and the host's menu work as on a Bazzite headset PC with the same headset, and both runtimes close on Stop. Record the alignment check's answer.
9. Audio: play game audio in a session (and speak, if *Headset microphone* is on). It passes when the sound plays where SteamVR's audio settings send it (the headset's own output, where it has one), with *Headset microphone* on the microphone reaches the rendering PC's virtual cable, and after Stop Windows' default devices are as before. VROSTBridge itself changes no audio device on Windows; the page names the current defaults.
10. Tray: with the page closed, start a session from the tray icon's **Start session** and stop it with **Stop session**. It passes when both work, the tooltip shows the session's state, and Windows shows a *Streaming* notification once the picture arrives. A tray-started session that ends on its own (end `VROSTBridgeClient.exe` in Task Manager, for example) must show *Session stopped* with the reason, and one that cannot start (rendering PC off) *Session did not start* with the reason.
11. Start at sign-in: turn on *Start VROSTBridge Client when I sign in* (in the installer or on the page), then sign out and in. It passes when only the tray icon appears (no browser, no SteamVR) and a session starts from it; with the switch off it no longer starts at sign-in. If Windows' own *Startup apps* setting has turned it off, the page must say so.
12. Wi-Fi: on Wi-Fi, run a session of at least 15 minutes. It passes when the client's log has `WIFI … Media streaming mode is on for this session` and `WIFI ROAMING locked: Wi-Fi background scans are off for this session`, the timeline shows no stall every few minutes, and after Stop the log says *Wi-Fi media streaming mode ended* and *Wi-Fi background scans are back on*, with any other Wi-Fi adapter on the same network reconnected. On Windows 11 24H2 or later, with location access for desktop apps off, the page must show *Windows didn’t allow reading the Wi-Fi network name; roaming detection is off* while the session streams normally.
13. Power: set Windows' power mode (Settings → System → Power & battery) to anything but *Best performance*, and note your power plan. It passes when the mode reads *Best performance* during a session, the PC and its display stay on through a long session without keyboard or mouse use, and your own mode is back after Stop. Then force a crash: during a session, end VROSTBridge Client's process tree in Task Manager (close SteamVR too if it stays open) and start VROSTBridge Client again. It passes when your mode comes back at that start, with the log line `POWER recovered: Windows' power mode is back to …`. With the High or Ultimate performance plan the mode must be left alone.
14. Baballonia's Windows installation: close Bigscreen's own eye-tracking app, turn on *Eye tracking* and set up Baballonia, accepting its licence. It passes when about 423 MB downloads, the size and SHA-256 check passes, it installs without a window into `%LOCALAPPDATA%\VROSTBridge\Baballonia\1.1.1.0rc6`, no Baballonia window opens by itself when it finishes, no new Baballonia shortcut appears in the Start menu or on the desktop, and *Installed apps* (*Apps & features* on Windows 10) gains no Baballonia entry (one you had before stays as it was). Record how long it took.
15. Your own Baballonia: on a PC where Baballonia 1.1.1.0rc6 was installed with Project Babble's own installer, set up eye tracking. It passes when VROSTBridge uses that installation without downloading (the client's log says *Eye tracking: the Baballonia 1.1.1.0rc6 installed on this PC (…) is used; nothing was downloaded*), and both *Remove Baballonia* and uninstalling VROSTBridge Client leave it installed and working. Another Baballonia version must not be used: VROSTBridge downloads its own instead.
16. Calibration and gaze: start a session with *Eye tracking* on, choose **Show Baballonia** and run its *Eye* calibration, turning your head far enough each way (corners too) while you follow the dot. It passes when the eye camera is found (Windows normally names it `BigEye`; under another name, such as *USB Video Device*, the page and the log say how to fix that), the calibration shows in the headset over the stream, the session keeps running through calibration and training, the card shows 30 or more eye-tracking samples a second, the sharp region follows your eyes toward the edges, and *Gaze reach* then has no direction under 30°. Record the four *Gaze reach* numbers and the samples a second.
17. Stop: press **Stop** on the page. It passes when the native client stops within 8 s (`STATUS Client stopped by the companion` in the log), the log has `Requesting local SteamVR shutdown.` and then `Local SteamVR stopped.`, the host returns to waiting, and the power mode and Wi-Fi are back as before; SteamVR started on its own afterwards shows its home and dashboard again. `Local SteamVR did not stop; closing only verified session processes.` means SteamVR ignored its quit request: record it.
18. A compositor failure: during a session, end `vrcompositor.exe` in Task Manager. It passes when the log says *SteamVR's compositor on this PC closed unexpectedly*, the session restarts by itself, once (a second failure within ten minutes stops it with an explanation), and the log and report have `STEAMVR crash detail:` lines; a process ended this way leaves no crash record, so the line saying Windows recorded none is the expected one here.
19. Uninstall: uninstall VROSTBridge Client (from *Installed apps*, *Apps & features* on Windows 10, or its Start menu entry). It passes when Windows asks once for administrator permission to remove the firewall rules, no `VROSTBridge.Client.*` rule remains, the Run value `VROSTBridgeClient` is gone (*Startup apps* no longer lists VROSTBridge Client), the program folder and its Start menu entries are gone, settings, pairing and logs stay in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\VROSTBridge`, Baballonia is kept unless you chose to remove it, and a VROSTBridge Host on the same PC is untouched. Declining the administrator prompt must leave VROSTBridge Client installed, with a message saying why.

As above, each check counts when its result repeats; for anything that fails, send both PCs' reports and say at which step and which log line it failed.
